# German: writing correct German, every time

*The German checklist referred to by `rules/language-quality.md`. Perfect, exact, professional German for every Anschreiben, Lebenslauf line and portal answer, whatever the owner's own level.*

## Formality

Formal *Sie/Ihnen/Ihr* throughout, capitalised, no switch to *du* even if the job ad uses *du* (mirroring a casual tone is a structure question for `applications.md`, not a grammar one). No contractions (*habe*,
never *hab*), no colloquialisms or filler (*halt*, *eigentlich*, *irgendwie*). Sentences may start with *Und* or *Aber* only rarely, for a deliberate short emphatic line.

## Core grammar checks: verify every one of these on every paragraph

1. **Every noun is capitalised**, including nominalised verbs and adjectives (*das Lesen*, *das Wichtigste*). A missed capital on a noun is the most visible non-native tell.
2. **Verb position.** Main clause: the finite verb is the **second element** (V2), not necessarily the second word (`Im letzten Jahr habe ich ...`). Subordinate clause (after *dass, weil, ob, wenn, da, obwohl,
   während*, a relative pronoun): the finite verb goes to the **end**. Separable-prefix verbs (*teilnehmen, durchführen, auswerten*): the prefix splits off to the end of the main clause (`Ich werte die Daten aus`),
   stays attached in a subordinate clause or an infinitive (`..., dass ich die Daten auswerte`).
3. **Case government.** Dative verbs without an accusative object: *helfen, danken, gratulieren, folgen, gefallen*. Two-way prepositions (*an, auf, in, hinter, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen*): accusative for motion
   into a state (*Ich gehe in die Abteilung*), dative for static location (*Ich arbeite in der Abteilung*). Always accusative: *durch, für, gegen, ohne, um*. Always dative: *aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu*. Genitive:
   *während, trotz, wegen, innerhalb*, and for possession (*die Leitung des Labors*): keep the genitive in written application German.
4. **Adjective endings** change with case, gender, number and what precedes: after *der/die/das* weak endings (-e/-en); after *ein/kein/mein* mixed; with no article strong endings. Check every adjective-noun pair
   individually.
5. **Gender and plural are memorised facts**, not guessable from an English cognate. Do not default a plural to *+s* (*Prozess, Prozesse*; *Projekt, Projekte*; *Auswertung, Auswertungen*).
6. **Perfect tense:** *sein* for motion or a change of state (*ich bin gegangen*), *haben* for everything else (*ich habe ausgewertet*). Application German uses Präteritum for a few fixed verbs (*war, hatte, konnte*) and
   Perfekt for the rest: do not mix registers within one paragraph.
7. **Compound nouns are one word** (*Datenmanagement*, not *Daten-Management*; *Arzneimittelsicherheit*). A hyphen is acceptable only when a long compound is genuinely hard to parse (*Pharmakovigilanz-Datenbank*).

## Spelling: ß, ss and umlauts

Real documents use proper umlauts and ß, never the ASCII transliteration. ß follows a long vowel or diphthong (*groß*, *außerdem*, *Straße*); ss follows a short vowel (*dass, Ergebnisse,
wusste*). Write standard (not Swiss) German unless the posting is Swiss, where ß is not used.

## Common non-native mistakes to check

- *seit* (ongoing, present tense: *Ich arbeite seit zwei Jahren ...*) vs *für* (a bounded duration: *Ich habe zwei Jahre lang gearbeitet*).
- *wenn* (conditional or repeated) vs *als* (a single past occurrence): *Als ich bei der Firma anfing ...*.
- Relative pronoun: gender and number from the noun it refers to, **case from its own clause** (*das Projekt, das ich geleitet habe*: accusative, object of *geleitet*).
- Modal verb plus infinitive: the infinitive goes to the end (*Ich möchte die Daten auswerten*).
- Do not borrow English sentence rhythm wholesale; a long sentence with a wrong verb position is worse than two short correct ones.
- Avoid Denglisch where a real word exists in formal register (*Bewerbung*, not *Applikation*; *Lebenslauf*, not *Curriculum Vitae* as running text; *Erfahrung*, not *Experience*).

## Salutation, closing and stock phrases for an Anschreiben

`Sehr geehrte Frau <Name>` / `Sehr geehrter Herr <Name>` when the posting names a contact (check the posting: it usually has one); `Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren` only when it genuinely does not. Close
`Mit freundlichen Grüßen` (with ü and ß, as in real documents). Never open with "Hiermit bewerbe ich mich". Some postings ask for no letter at all ("nur Lebenslauf hochladen"): honour that.
