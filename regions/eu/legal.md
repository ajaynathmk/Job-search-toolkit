# Europe: working across borders as a non-EU student or graduate

*Not legal advice. Each route must be confirmed with the host country's authority (embassy, immigration office) and your university's International
Office before an application is built around it. If you hold EU citizenship, none of this applies. The route adjustments in regions/eu/region.json
apply the consequences to a role's score; a role is never dropped because of its route.*

## Schengen and the EU mobility rules

| Rule | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Visits up to 90 days in any 180, **no work** | Art. 21 Schengen Implementing Convention; Reg. (EU) 2016/399 Art. 6; Reg. (EU) 2018/1806 | A residence permit from one Schengen state lets you **visit** the others, not take a job there. |
| Work or stay beyond 90 days needs the host state's own permit | BAMF "Mobility in the EU" | A paid internship or student job abroad needs the **host country's** authorisation. Your home permit's work allowance applies only in the home country. |
| Student mobility, up to 360 days per Member State | Art. 31 Directive (EU) 2016/801 | A student with a valid permit from the first state, covered by a Union or multilateral programme with mobility measures (Erasmus+) or by an agreement between higher-education institutions, may do part of their studies in a second state for up to 360 days. The second state may require notification and may object within 30 days. A student not covered must apply for a permit in the second state. |
| Work during studies | Art. 24 Directive (EU) 2016/801 | Outside study time and under the host state's rules students may be employed; each state sets a weekly maximum of at least 15 hours. Prior authorisation may be required. |
| Directive not applied | Directive recitals | **Denmark and Ireland** do not apply Directive 2016/801; their rules are purely national. Switzerland and the UK are outside it too. |

**Open question that the Directive does not settle:** whether an Erasmus+ traineeship or a thesis at a company counts as "part of their studies in a
higher education institution" in a given state. States split: some treat Erasmus traineeships as study mobility, others as employment needing a work
authorisation. Treat every placement abroad as "needs the host state's written answer" until the host state or your university says otherwise.

## The routes, ranked by how usable they are for a non-EU student

| # | Route | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Erasmus+ traineeship through your university | Enrolled student; the permit of the study country must stay valid for the whole stay; mandatory vs voluntary placement and the host country change the rules. Ask your International Office first. |
| 2 | EU Directive 2016/801 student/trainee mobility | Permit from the first state plus an exchange programme or university agreement, up to 360 days. The host state may ask for notification or its own permit. |
| 3 | Employer-sponsored skilled-worker permit | After graduating, or earlier for a degree-level job. Salary thresholds and recognised-sponsor schemes differ per country. |
| 4 | EU Blue Card, then move | After a qualifying job in the first state a Blue Card holder may move after a period of residence (read the current rule on the official pages) and apply for a new Blue Card; the new state's salary threshold applies. |
| 5 | The study country itself after graduation | Many countries give graduates a time-limited permit to look for work: this is the fallback that always exists. |
