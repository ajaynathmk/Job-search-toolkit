# Resume and cover-letter conventions: United States

| Item | Convention |
|---|---|
| Name | **Resume** (a "CV" in the U.S. means a long academic document; use it only for research and faculty positions). One page for students and early-career applicants, two pages at most with ten or more years of experience. |
| Photo and personal details | No photo, no date of birth, no marital status, no nationality. Employers must not ask. City and state are enough; a full street address is not expected. |
| Work authorization | Do not put your visa on the resume. Answer the application's "authorized to work / will you require sponsorship" questions truthfully and exactly: a student on F-1 with OPT eligibility is authorized to work now under OPT and may need sponsorship later. |
| Structure | Header, Summary (two lines, optional), Education (at the top for students), Experience, Projects, Skills. Bullets start with a verb and lead with the result, with numbers where you have them. |
| Cover letter | One page, "Dear Hiring Manager," or the named person, closing "Sincerely,". Say why this role and this employer in the first paragraph. Many portals make it optional: send one when you can write it specifically. |
| Spelling and dates | American spelling; dates as "Jan 2025" or "01/2025"; GPA only if it is 3.5 or higher (for new graduates). |
| Salary | Do not state an expectation unless the form asks; then give a range. |
| Portals | Most employers use an applicant tracking system (Greenhouse, Lever, Workday): use a plain single-column layout, standard headings and no text in images. |
