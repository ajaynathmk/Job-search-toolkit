# Germany: what a non-EU student or graduate may legally do

*Not legal advice. The local Ausländerbehörde, the university's International Office and the Bundesagentur
decide each case. The figures and wording below were read from the primary texts named in each row; the
region pack's route rules (regions/de/region.json) apply the scoring consequences. If you are an EU citizen,
none of this applies to you and the toolkit adds no route adjustment.*

## Student work

| Rule | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 140 days | § 16b Abs. 3 Satz 1 AufenthG (gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__16b.html) | A student residence permit allows employment up to 140 working days a year (the "Arbeitstagekonto"), or 280 half days. |
| Student side jobs are free | § 16b Abs. 3 Satz 2 | "Studentische Nebentaetigkeiten werden nicht angerechnet." Work as a student or research assistant (HiWi / SHK / WHK) at a university or research institution uses no work days. Confirm the wording with the Ausländerbehörde before relying on it for a non-university assistant job. |
| Part-time counting | § 16b Abs. 3 Saetze 3-5 | Counted the way that is more favourable, week by week: a day of up to 4 hours is a half day; or up to 20 hours a week during lectures (and any hours outside lecture time) count as 2.5 work days. |
| Mandatory internship | § 16b Abs. 1 (study purpose) | An internship the study programme requires is part of the study purpose, not employment on the account. A voluntary internship counts on the account. |
| Thesis at a company | not stated in § 16b | The thesis is mandatory, so it is study. A paid thesis contract is employment-law territory: ask the Ausländerbehörde whether it counts before signing. |

One 20 h/week working-student job all year counts 2.5 days a week, 52 x 2.5 = 130 days, inside the 140. Two such jobs do not fit.
Assistant jobs at a university add nothing to the count, so they are the cheapest way to use the account. Count by calendar year and keep payslips.

## After graduating

| Route | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 18-month job-search permit | § 20 AufenthG (make-it-in-germany.com) | For graduates of a German university: time to find a job that fits the degree. Check on the official page what work is allowed meanwhile. |
| EU Blue Card | § 18g AufenthG | Qualifying degree plus a job offer above the salary threshold (a lower threshold applies to shortage occupations and recent graduates). Thresholds change every year: read the current figures at make-it-in-germany.com. |
| Skilled-worker permit | § 18a / § 18b AufenthG | Vocational or academic qualification and a matching job offer. |

## Moving elsewhere in Schengen

A German student permit lets you visit other Schengen states for up to 90 days in any 180 (Art. 21 Schengen Implementing Convention), **without working**.
A job or paid internship abroad needs the host state's own authorisation. Directive (EU) 2016/801 Art. 31 gives students covered by Erasmus+ or an agreement between
universities a mobility right of up to 360 days per Member State; whether a company thesis or a traineeship counts as "part of their studies" is decided state by state.
See regions/eu/ for the country-by-country notes.
